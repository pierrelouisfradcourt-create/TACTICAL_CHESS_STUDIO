## WORLD SCAN OBSERVATION: TETRIS & COMPARABLE PUZZLE GAMES

### TETRIS (Official Guideline)

Tetris operates under the official **Tetris Guideline** standard, documented across multiple authoritative sources. The core mechanic combines **7-bag randomizer** (all 7 tetrominoes shuffled, max 12-piece drought for any single piece type) with **Super Rotation System (SRS)** using a 5-point wall-kick algorithm. Players can hold one piece, preview the next 5 pieces, and use soft/hard drop mechanics.

**Gameplay Progression by Time Horizon:**
- **Minute 1**: Piece spawn, basic rotation (SRS), position first I/O/T tetrominoes, clear opening line.
- **Minute 10**: 5–8 lines cleared, level speed increase triggered, height management becomes critical, hold and preview mechanics in active use.
- **Hour 5**: Intermediate player executing T-spin doubles and back-to-back Tetrises, managing parity (well structure), speedrun lines-per-minute optimization.
- **Endgame**: Top-out (grid height reaches spawn zone), game over. In marathon: score maxes. In sprint: time recorded.

**Retention Drivers**: Progressive difficulty (speed escalation per level) creates a natural skill gate. Accessible entry (stacking fundamentals taught first) feeds into advanced mastery (T-spin combos, perfect clears). High-score chasing and competitive speedrun leaderboards drive repeated play. Fair randomization (7-bag eliminates perceived RNG frustration). Clear visual/audio feedback on line clears reinforces completion feedback loop.

### PUYO PUYO (Versus Puzzle / Story Mode)

Puyo Puyo fundamentally inverts Tetris: instead of lines, players match 4+ puyos (colored blobs) adjacent to each other. The **chain mechanic** creates cascades when cleared puyos fall and form new matches. Crucially, chains send **garbage** to opponent's board (top-down pressure, opposite of Tetris bottom-up). **Offsetting** (counter-chaining to neutralize garbage) creates defensive gameplay depth. **Fever mode** (continuous chain state) provides euphoric power fantasy.

**Gameplay Progression by Time Horizon:**
- **Minute 1**: Place first puyo pair, learn 4-match clear, first small chain sends garbage to opponent.
- **Minute 10**: Opponent's garbage arrives, player learns offsetting (damage mitigation), Fever gauge builds, managing board height under pressure.
- **Hour 5**: Executing 4–5 chain sequences, reading opponent's board state, timing clears to avoid garbage avalanche, Fever mode activation (automatic offset), character ability bonuses active.
- **Endgame**: Opponent top-out (stack filled to spawn line) = victory. OR All Clear (zero puyos remaining) = 30-line garbage dump to opponent (comeback mechanic).

**Retention Drivers**: Asymmetric competition (attacker chains vs defender offsets creates chaos). Risk/reward: small opponent chain can completely disrupt player setup. Story mode with **permadeath** (ally lost permanently if defeated while playing that character) creates emotional stakes and roster management strategy. Fever mode visual/sensory overload + power fantasy. Chain prediction requires foresight (non-obvious cascade paths), enabling skill expression and learning depth.

### PANEL DE PON (1995, Match-3 Puzzle / Story Mode)

Panel de Pon uses cursor-based swapping: player controls a rectangular cursor to swap two adjacent panels. Matching 3+ panels (horizontal/vertical) clears them; gravity applies, panels above fall, potential cascade (chain reaction from automatic new-match formation). Opponent sends **garbage panels** (gray blocks, cleared only by matching adjacent color). Story mode features **permadeath** for non-main characters, creating recruitment/benching strategy.

**Gameplay Progression by Time Horizon:**
- **Minute 1**: Cursor movement, panel swaps, form first 3-match, learn cascade concept.
- **Minute 10**: Stack rising at fixed speed, player chains combos (5+ cascades), opponent garbage pressure introduced, clearing garbage by matching adjacent.
- **Hour 5**: Large chains (7+ cascades), faster stack, character-specific abilities active (speed, damage multiplier, garbage rate), ally tactical swaps mid-battle.
- **Endgame**: Opponent stack top-out = victory. Player stack top-out = defeat. Story: advance to next encounter. Endless: end.

**Retention Drivers**: Character-driven story narrative with permadeath mechanics (ally loss is permanent, encourages strategic benching). Multiple modes (story/endless/timed/puzzle) for different play sessions. Cascade visual spectacle (explosions, screen shake, color flashes) provides kinetic satisfaction. Difficulty escalation per story encounter. Character ability variety enables tactical roster composition (customizable strategy per ally).

## COMPARATIVE ARCHITECTURE

All three games employ **progressive difficulty escalation** but via different mechanics:
- **Tetris**: Speed increase (time pressure). No opponent.
- **Puyo Puyo**: Speed + asymmetric garbage attack. Direct opponent interaction via chain offense/defense.
- **Panel de Pon**: Stack height + character ability modifiers. Story narrative framing (combat encounters as chapters).

**Shared Core Loop Structure**:
1. **Input** (rotate/position piece or swap panels)
2. **Gravity** (pieces/panels fall)
3. **Match Detection** (line clear or 4-match clear or 3-match clear)
4. **Cascade** (new pieces/panels fall, form new matches, potential chain)
5. **Score/Feedback** (points, visual feedback, opponent pressure or progression)
6. **Escalation** (speed/difficulty increases, new challenge introduced)

**Economy Patterns**:
- **Tetris**: Points = lines cleared × level multiplier. Progression gate: lines-to-clear per level.
- **Puyo Puyo**: Damage = chain length (exponential scaling). Garbage economy: larger chain = more garbage to opponent. All Clear = instant 30-line pressure reversal.
- **Panel de Pon**: Points = combo multiplier (cascades build multiplier). Story progression = encounter sequence. Ally roster = persistent progression.
