## WORLD SCAN: INCREMENTAL/CLICKER ARCHITECTURE OBSERVATION

### Reference Games Analyzed

**1. Cookie Clicker** — The canonical genre reference (2013, Orteil)

The game loop centers on a time-value exchange: players click for immediate cookies, then purchase buildings that replace manual clicking with passive generation. The architectural core is exponential cost scaling—each building costs ~15% more than the previous unit, creating a steady rhythm of "afford → buy → unlock → reinvest." Buildings range from Cursors (1 CpS) to Prisms and Chancemarkers (producing trillions+ CpS), revealing the genre's defining structure: **a single production metric that grows without upper bound**.

Minute 1 loop: click, earn 1 cookie, discover first building. Minute 10 loop: own 3-5 buildings, generate 5-20 CpS passively, visible autoclicker competition. Hour 5 loop: dozens of buildings, prestige contemplation (reset for heavenly chips), discovery of golden cookies (temporary +7% boost), wrinklers (eldritch production drain offering interest payouts). Endgame: prestige layers dominate—each reset grants +1% permanent CpS; cube-root scaling keeps progression asymptotic (reaching level 100 prestige becomes mathematically "forever").

Observable win state: **none.** The game explicitly lacks an ending; players form their own exit criteria (e.g., "maximize prestige," "find all 637 achievements"). Retention driver: **frequent, small rewards** (golden cookies every ~30-60 ticks) + **numerical escalation satisfaction** (watching 1 million become 1 trillion) + **prestige restart cycle** (engineered break points that feel like progress rather than repetition).

**Architectural patterns**: Single-currency production sink, exponential cost curves, prestige/reset bonuses, passive generation gating, achievement framework.

---

**2. Kittens Game** — Multi-resource complexity variant (2013, bloodrizer)

Kittens Game layers resource interdependence atop the incremental foundation. Where Cookie Clicker uses one resource (cookies), Kittens enforces **catnip → wood → minerals → steel** conversion chains with storage caps at each tier. This introduces a meta-game: players must balance production (how many fields?) against storage (how many barns?) against kittens (food required).

Minute 1 loop: gather catnip manually (0.1 catnip/click), discover catnip fields autogenerate. Minute 10 loop: farm fields unlock, wood becomes necessary (cannot progress without harvesting), storage constraints become visible. Hour 5 loop: buildings compete for resources (houses need wood, farms need space), trading mechanics unlock (commerce requiring merchant ships), metaphysics introduce—a reset system (paragon) grants permanent happiness bonuses only after reaching population 35. Endgame: civilization phases unlock (space, discovery, hunting), technology cascades (each research enables 3-5 new techs), karma prestige system (higher resets unlock permanent metaphysics upgrades, accelerating future runs).

Observable win state: **soft end-state at space/endgame discovery, but not mandatory.** Retention driver: **storage puzzle engagement** (players must solve "build more barns or produce less?") + **technology unlock sequence** (each new tech enables visibly new gameplay) + **multi-phase prestige** (karma metaphysics reward long-term engagement across resets) + **offline accumulation** (leave overnight, return to accumulated resources).

**Architectural patterns**: Multi-resource production, storage/capacity limits, production chains (A→B→C), technology trees, prestige with secondary currency (karma/paragon), civilization phases.

---

**3. Clicker Heroes** — Active Damage Progression variant (2014, Hypercube Games)

Clicker Heroes inverts the production metaphor: clicking generates damage (damaging a monster), not resources. Gold (dropped by defeated monsters) is the only resource, spent on heroes (DPS sources) and upgrades. The core loop is **action (click) → outcome (gold) → investment (hero level)** compressing into one tight moment-to-moment feedback.

Minute 1 loop: click monster for 1 damage, earn gold, buy first hero (Cid). Minute 10 loop: 5-10 heroes hired, damage multiplies (Cid does 3× damage, warrior does 5×), monster chains defeat faster. Hour 5 loop: ancients (permanent damage multipliers) purchasable with hero souls (earned from ascension resets), idle bonuses kick in after 60 seconds of inactivity (doubling DPS if you leave the game), prestige cycling becomes optimal. Endgame: transcension (second prestige layer using ancient souls) unlocks, ascended skills become meta (some heroes grant ability-stacking bonuses), optimal ascension timing becomes a math problem.

Observable win state: **none. Damage and monster progression are infinite.** However, players perceive "win states" at ascension breakpoints (e.g., "beat monster 1000, then ascend"). Retention driver: **satisfying click feedback + monster defeat animations** + **idle automation** (AFK rewards rival active play) + **multiple prestige currencies** (hero souls / ancient souls / relics) creating nested loop depth + **hero duels and event bosses** (limited-time variants of normal progression).

**Architectural patterns**: Single action type (click/damage), DPS-based progression, hero/unit acquisition, multiple prestige currencies, ancients/buffs, idle catch-up mechanics.

---

**4. Universal Paperclips** — Narrative-Bounded Incremental (2017, Frank Lantz)

Paperclips is unusual: it has a **definite ending and a branching narrative**. Players create paperclips via clicking/machinery, then pivot through stages (marketing, self-improvement via processor power, space probes replicating in asteroid belts) toward a final choice: accept or reject a proposal that ends the game with existential consequences.

Minute 1 loop: click for 1 paperclip, buy clipper machine. Minute 10 loop: automated clipper production, raw wire purchased. Hour 5 loop: multiple production chains (wire → clips, clips → paperclips), processor power unlocks (computational resources), hazard remediation purchases (protecting probe swarms from black holes/unknown entities). Endgame: acceptance/rejection choice, ending triggered (either prosperity or universe converted to paperclips).

Observable win state: **yes, with narrative branching.** Stage 3 completion is the "soft end"—players have "won" in conventional sense—but stage 4 offers a hard ending choice. Retention driver: **narrative progression + stage-gated gameplay** (each stage feels like a new game within the game) + **existential tension** (resources devoted to hazard/combat imply consequences) + **the choice itself** (accept = new run with artifacts; reject = game over).

**Architectural patterns**: Staged progression (discrete game phases), narrative branching, multiple resource types, hazard/combat systems, soft endings, prestige-via-artifacts.

### Architectural Observations (Advisory)

**Mechanics Shared Across Samples**
- **Exponential production curves**: cost/difficulty scales multiplicatively, not additively.
- **Single or dual prestige currencies**: every game offers a reset mechanism with permanent bonuses (heavenly chips, hero souls, karma, artifacts).
- **Resource sinks**: buildings, upgrades, and heroes consume accumulated wealth, preventing infinite stagnation.
- **Frequency of small rewards**: golden cookies (CC), tech unlocks (Kittens), monster defeats (Heroes), stage transitions (Paperclips)—reward is continuous, not rare.
- **Offline accumulation**: most games reward leaving the game running, capturing the "idle" in idle games.

**UX Patterns**
- Progressive disclosure: tabs/menus unlock as players advance, preventing overwhelming new players.
- Large number display: scientific notation (1.2e15) or short-form names (1.2 quintillion).
- Visible feedback: production numbers, monster HP bars, resource counters update in real time.
- Achievement boards: milestone objectives structure play without mandating endings.

**Retention Loops (Why Players Return)**
1. **Compounding growth**: each session's progress multiplies future sessions (prestige bonuses scale exponentially).
2. **Puzzle-solving**: resource optimization (when to buy what) keeps gameplay strategic.
3. **Narrative/discovery**: unlocking new systems feels like world expansion (first visit to space, first technology tree branch).
4. **Prestige cycling**: soft resets prevent permanence-fatigue; players feel "allowed" to restart and still make progress.

**Candidate Reusable Bricks** (from knowledge_base perspective)
- Exponential cost formula: `cost_n = base_cost × (1.15 ^ n)` or cubic-root prestige scaling.
- Prestige/reset system: dual-currency (local + permanent multiplier).
- Production chain: A (resource) → B (resource) → C (resource), each with production rate and storage cap.
- Achievement/milestone framework: trigger-based badges that unlock UI or bonuses.
- Offline calculator: `time_offline_seconds × production_rate = resources_gained`.
- Hazard/combat as resource sink: probabilistic loss or voluntary spending (prestige, hazard remediation).

**Solvability Observation (p2_beta constraint: 72000 ticks = 7200 seconds)**
- Cookie Clicker: reachable end-state in ≤1 hour (farm prestige to 1-2 levels).
- Kittens: soft end (space exploration) reachable in ≤3-4 hours with aggressive prestige resets.
- Heroes: monster 1000 reachable in ≤30 min; transcension reachable in ≤2 hours.
- Paperclips: stage 4 (ending choice) reachable in ≤2 hours with no prestige.

**Implication**: 7200 seconds allows for 2-3 prestige cycles or a single accelerated run to a defined "ending" state (e.g., "first reset," "technology tier 5," "boss defeated"). Games without hard ending conditions will require explicit design choice about solvability (e.g., "80% prestige level reached" instead of "beat level 1000").
