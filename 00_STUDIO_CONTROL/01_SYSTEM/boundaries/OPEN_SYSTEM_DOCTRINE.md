# OPEN_SYSTEM_DOCTRINE

status: DOCUMENTED_ONLY

## Purpose

This document defines a studio-wide structural doctrine:

Open systems preserve feedback, contradiction, plurality, review, living systems, and bounded change.
Closed systems suppress feedback, centralize authority, overfit to their own outputs, consume their environment as fuel, and become brittle.

The studio must favor open-system structure over closed-system structure.

## Core Principle

The central opposition is not human versus AI, tool versus operator, or automation versus manual work.

The central opposition is:

- open systems
- closed systems

An open system remains connected to reality through feedback, evidence, review, dissent, reversible action, and external checks.

A closed system treats its own model, output, status, or authority as sufficient proof. It reduces contradiction, hides uncertainty, and tends toward self-confirming decisions.

The studio treats living systems as part of the real feedback substrate. A system that destroys the living world, exhausts its material base, or enters an unbounded expansion loop is structurally closed even if it remains computationally powerful.

## Structural Requirements

- HumanGate remains the activation authority for irreversible, high-risk, public, dataset, model, merge, push, PR, benchmark, and claim decisions.
- Search remains final gameplay authority where gameplay legality or tactical truth is involved.
- Neural systems may propose, rank, summarize, or assist, but must not become sole authority.
- Reports, logs, latest.json, benchmarks, and runtime artifacts are observations, not truth.
- Claims require evidence packages and explicit claim scope.
- Decisions must preserve provenance, reviewability, and rollback paths where practical.
- Uncertainty must be represented instead of hidden.
- Systems must avoid unbounded expansion loops that convert resources, attention, compute, datasets, or living environments into growth for its own sake.
- Systems must preserve living feedback sources instead of destroying, enclosing, or exhausting them.

## Open-System Markers

- Multiple evidence sources can challenge a conclusion.
- Human review can reject automated output.
- Status fields distinguish code, tests, runtime artifacts, canonical docs, roadmap, and inference.
- Fail-closed boundaries prevent weak evidence from becoming authority.
- Docs distinguish implemented behavior from documented-only intent.
- New doctrine can be revised through explicit review instead of silent drift.
- Growth is bounded by purpose, evidence, resource limits, and HumanGate.
- Living systems are treated as feedback-bearing conditions to preserve, not raw material to consume.

## Closed-System Warnings

- A model cites its own output as proof.
- A benchmark is treated as production readiness.
- A roadmap item is treated as implementation.
- A generated dataset bypasses ActionId, LegalAction, ActionMask, provenance, or HumanGate.
- A passive adapter is treated as an active controller.
- A convenience script silently becomes canonical runtime behavior.
- A system suppresses contradiction to look stable.
- A system pursues expansion, compute, data collection, automation, or optimization without a bounded purpose.
- A system treats living systems, users, culture, or environment as expendable inputs.

## Scope Limits

This doctrine does not activate agents, training, benchmarks, datasets, Chess960, ActionMask implementation, DecisionController activation, network behavior, publishing, claims, or autonomous runtime behavior.

This doctrine is a structural reference for how the studio should separate evidence, authority, feedback, and action.

It also records a non-runtime ecological constraint: intelligence must not justify destructive expansion loops against the living systems that make open feedback possible.
